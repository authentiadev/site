# Authentia service expansion: research and recommendation

Research date: **29 October 2026**. Internal working document; not a public case study or a claim of completed client work.

## Recommendation

Make **AI & Digital Change Management** a core offering. Organise the surrounding services into six connected areas: AI strategy; AI and digital change management; workflow and operating model design; practical AI governance; implementation and pilot advisory; and ongoing AI and digital change advisory.

This extends Authentia's existing applied-AI positioning naturally. The opportunity is to help organisations decide what to change, prepare the people affected, establish ownership, and evaluate progress. Authentia supplies analysis, facilitation, plans and decision support; clients and their chosen delivery partners retain execution and operational responsibility.

Make **AI value and investment reviews** an explicit starting point within strategy and ongoing advisory. Embed leadership alignment and AI literacy in change management. Place vendor comparisons and pilot reviews within implementation advisory. This gives buyers clear ways to begin without creating a separate service for every related task.

## How to interpret the evidence

- **Consulting-buyer preferences** describe what respondents want from advisers. They are the closest evidence of service demand here, but still do not establish awarded contracts or demand specifically for Authentia.
- **Investment intentions** indicate planned spending on technology or AI. They are not necessarily spending on external advice, and plans may change.
- **Adoption and governance gaps** describe current conditions. A gap can make a service useful without proving that organisations will buy it.
- **Our recommendations** connect those findings with Authentia's documents and the owner's stated consulting model. They are commercial judgments, not survey results.

The sources below are original research or official statistics. Each has a disclosed sample or method. Consulting and technology providers have a commercial interest in the subject; government research provides a useful independent counterweight. Most findings are self-reported. None should be presented as a universal result for all mid-sized or large companies in Canada, the US and the UK.

## Recommended service architecture

| Service | Buyer problem and useful outputs | Responsibility boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Strategy** | Prioritise opportunities against business needs, readiness, cost and risk. Outputs: opportunity assessment, use-case portfolio, business case, sequencing, roadmap and decision brief. **AI value and investment reviews** can start a new engagement or challenge an existing initiative's assumptions. | Recommendations and documented assumptions; client approves investments and owns delivery. No promised ROI. |
| **AI & Digital Change Management** | Prepare people to adopt a changed tool or way of working. Outputs: stakeholder and change-impact assessment, sponsor alignment, readiness review, role-based enablement plan, communications plan, adoption measures and ownership map. Leadership AI literacy sits here. | Advisory and facilitation; client sponsors, people managers and delivery teams execute the programme and manage employees. |
| **Workflow & Operating Model Design** | Make changes to work explicit before expanding tool use. Outputs: current/future workflow maps, human/AI handoffs, decision rights, exception routes, role implications and prioritised operating-model recommendations. | Design and recommendations; client validates feasibility and implements organisational or system changes. |
| **Practical AI Governance** | Clarify acceptable use, ownership and review decisions. Outputs: AI-use inventory approach, policy framework, decision and escalation routes, review criteria, human oversight requirements and governance operating rhythm. | Business governance advice coordinated with client legal, privacy, security and risk owners. No legal opinions, security certification or guarantee of compliance. |
| **Implementation & Pilot Advisory** | Help client teams choose a feasible path and make informed pilot decisions. Outputs: requirements brief, build/buy comparison, vendor evaluation criteria, pilot design, success measures, readiness review, issue analysis and go/no-go recommendation. Vendor and pilot reviews live here. | Client or selected partners build, deploy, integrate, support and operate systems. Use “review” or “decision support”; avoid “assurance,” which can imply an audit-level guarantee. |
| **Ongoing AI & Digital Change Advisory** | Keep priorities, adoption and investment decisions current. Outputs: decision sessions, roadmap reviews, adoption/benefits reviews, recommendations as tools change, and support for leadership decisions. **AI value and investment reviews** are an explicit recurring or standalone entry point. | A scoped advisory relationship, not a managed AI service, programme ownership or an accountable corporate officer appointment. |

The six services should share a single story: choose valuable changes, shape how work will operate, prepare people and governance, support delivery decisions, and review what happens. A client can enter at any stage. They do not need to buy a complete transformation programme.

## Buyers and buying triggers

The following roles and triggers are **our targeting hypotheses**, inferred from the subject matter and organisational responsibilities. They are not counts of buyers in the cited surveys.

| Likely buyer or sponsor | Recognisable trigger | Relevant entry point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CIO, technology leader, head of AI | Many experiments, unclear priorities, overlapping tools or pressure to justify investment | Strategy, value review, vendor/pilot review |
| COO, operations or business-unit leader | AI introduced into work but responsibilities, exceptions or benefits remain unclear | Workflow/operating model design, value review |
| Transformation or programme leader | A rollout lacks readiness, ownership or a practical adoption plan | Change readiness and adoption planning, pilot review |
| HR, people, learning or organisational-change leader | Generic training is not translating into confident role-level use | Change impact, role-based enablement and leadership alignment |
| Risk, privacy, governance or legal leader | Unclear acceptable use, review gates or responsibility across functions | Practical governance with the client's specialist owners |
| Executive sponsor or finance partner | A pilot needs a business decision or benefits claims need scrutiny | Investment review, success measures and go/no-go support |

Prioritise organisations with an existing leadership team and internal or partner delivery capacity. This is a better fit for Authentia's model than companies seeking a supplier to own all implementation. Market Canada, the US and the UK as service geographies without manufacturing local offices, regional credentials or region-specific delivery experience.

## Concrete engagement starting points

These are proposed packages, not assertions that Authentia has already delivered them. Agree scope, access, responsibilities and outputs before quoting; do not publish invented standard prices or timelines.

- **AI Value & Investment Review:** examine an existing proposal or initiative; document the baseline, benefit assumptions, likely costs, readiness dependencies and evidence needed for the next decision. Finish with a recommendation and measurement plan. Available through strategy or ongoing advisory.
- **AI & Digital Change Readiness Review:** assess affected stakeholders, sponsorship, workflow/role changes, readiness and adoption risks. Finish with a prioritised change plan, owners and measures. Include leadership alignment and role-specific enablement needs.
- **Workflow Design Workshop:** map a selected process, identify decisions and exceptions, evaluate suitable AI involvement, and document a future workflow with human oversight and handoffs. Finish with a delivery brief for the client's team.
- **Practical AI Governance Review:** examine the current use landscape, ownership and policies; identify proportionate review routes and produce a governance action plan for client legal, privacy, security and business owners to validate.
- **Vendor or Pilot Decision Review:** compare defined options against requirements, inspect the evidence available, identify open questions and recommend proceed, revise, pause or stop. A favourable review is not a technical certification or a guarantee of safe deployment.
- **Ongoing Decision Support:** use agreed review points to revisit priorities, value assumptions, adoption evidence and emerging issues. State the decisions supported and response expectations in the engagement scope.

Useful resources can support these entry points: a change-readiness checklist, stakeholder/impact worksheet, sponsor briefing, role-based AI practice guide, benefits tracker and vendor/pilot questions. Prompt packs should teach users to check outputs and protect confidential information. They complement organisation-specific advice; they cannot establish readiness, governance compliance or successful adoption by themselves.

## Positioning and voice

Suggested positioning: **“Applied AI strategy and digital change advice for decisions your team can act on.”** Supporting copy can explain that Authentia connects business priorities, people, workflows and practical governance, then supports the client's teams and chosen partners through pilot decisions and ongoing review.

Keep the current visual identity and assets. Extend the offer through the existing service-page pattern, case studies where the evidence supports them, and substantive articles/resources. A new service does not require a new visual brand.

Use language such as “clarify priorities,” “prepare teams,” “map the workflow,” “define ownership,” “review the evidence” and “make the next decision.” Name the output and the owner of the next action. Describe leadership workshops as practical decision and alignment sessions, rather than promising to make every employee an AI expert.

Avoid sweeping claims about enterprise transformation, effortless adoption, guaranteed savings or proven change-programme outcomes. Do not turn positive but undocumented feedback into numbers, testimonials or causal benefits. Keep anonymous project descriptions factual, distinguish the completed scope from planned future rollout, and introduce new offers as what Authentia can help clients do.

## Keep the offer focused

Do not add ERP implementation, managed operations or cybersecurity execution simply because they are adjacent to digital transformation. They introduce different delivery capacity, ongoing operational obligations and proof requirements. Practical requirements or vendor advice may touch those topics within a scoped engagement; that does not justify selling the implementation service.

Do not advertise a fractional accountable officer or outsourced corporate AI owner without a deliberate business decision about authority, availability, liability and capability. The present model is an adviser supporting the client's accountable leaders.

Avoid a long list of overlapping services such as separate pages for leadership literacy, vendor selection, measurement, workshops and prompt engineering. The six-service architecture makes these capabilities discoverable in context, gives each a business purpose, and preserves a coherent brand. Expand further only when actual enquiry patterns, delivery evidence and capabilities justify it.

## Evidence and publication discipline

Research validates the relevance of a problem; Authentia's own verified records validate experience. Keep those forms of evidence separate. Do not imply that the organisations behind the public research are clients or endorsers. Do not publish confidential client names or document details.

Before presenting any specific outcome, verify its baseline, measurement period, source and attribution. For new service pages, explain approach, outputs and responsibility boundaries instead of inventing past projects. Describe governance as a practical business capability with specialist client review, and implementation advisory as advice through decisions and handoff. Client teams or appointed partners remain responsible for execution.
