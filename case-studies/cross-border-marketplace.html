Supporting a cross-border vehicle marketplace

Selected digital delivery experience supporting a vehicle marketplace connecting private sellers and dealerships across Canada and the United States.

Digital delivery experience · By Authentia · Updated October 28, 2026

Authentia supported development of a vehicle marketplace connecting private sellers and dealerships across Canada and the United States. The contribution centred on engineering collaboration and delivery coordination within the client's wider leadership structure. This is earlier digital product experience.

A vehicle transaction crosses several journeys: listing, bidding, offer acceptance, and the steps that follow. Supporting a marketplace built around that process meant working across mobile, web, and connected services.

One transaction, several connected journeys

The product brought together people with different needs. A vehicle owner needed a way to submit information and review offers. A dealership needed to assess a listing and participate in bidding. Once an offer was accepted, the platform needed to connect that decision with the subsequent transaction.

The intended experience joined mobile and web applications with real-time bidding, vehicle information, agreements, payment-related services, and logistics coordination. A change in one part of that journey could affect the work that followed it. The complexity lay in connecting the parts into a coherent product.

A practical delivery contribution

Authentia supported the engineering work and its coordination across the engagement. The broader client-side leadership retained ownership of product strategy and project direction. Our contribution focused on supporting the development effort and collaboration needed to bring the product's requirements into implementation.

The work spanned the consumer-facing mobile experience and the dealership-facing web environment. The documented product scope covered the following journey:

  1. Vehicle submission. A seller provides the information needed to present a vehicle for bidding.
  2. Dealership bidding. Participating dealerships review listings and submit bids through the platform.
  3. Offer review and acceptance. The seller considers an offer and proceeds through the application.
  4. Transaction coordination. Connected services support agreements, payment-related steps, and collection or drop-off arrangements.

Working across product boundaries

The platform combined application development with third-party integrations. Vehicle history, contract execution, and transaction-related services needed to fit into the wider journey. Mobile and web interfaces also needed to work with shared platform services and data.

This kind of delivery depends on understanding the boundaries between systems. The customer sees a continuous experience, while the implementation crosses applications and service providers. Coordinating the work requires attention to those connections as well as to the individual features.

The marketplace also served participants in Canada and the United States. That cross-border context formed part of the product's operational requirements and the environment in which its integrations needed to work.

Experience through launch and further development

The marketplace was developed and launched, with continuing work on maintenance and additional features following the initial release. Authentia's earlier delivery experience includes that transition from building a product to supporting its ongoing development.

This case reflects conventional software delivery. It demonstrates experience with connected customer journeys, application development, integration dependencies, and collaboration within an established project structure.
