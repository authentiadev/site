Implementation and pilot advisory

Shape a bounded pilot, evaluate tools and delivery plans, and help your team make informed implementation decisions.

Carry the strategy into real decisions

A promising use case introduces a new set of questions once a team starts planning implementation. Which capabilities should an existing product provide? What information should the system use? Where should a person review its output? What would justify expanding beyond a pilot?

Authentia supports the sponsor and delivery team through these decisions. We help translate the intended business outcome into a scope that a technical team or provider can assess, build, and evaluate.

Give the pilot a clear job

A pilot needs to resolve an uncertainty. It may test whether a product can use the right knowledge, whether a workflow fits daily operations, or whether review effort leaves a worthwhile benefit. Defining that purpose helps prevent a demonstration from becoming an open-ended implementation.

We help set the intended users, information sources, boundaries, and exceptions. A good pilot brief records what is included, what will wait, and what should happen when the proposed approach does not work.

Make tool and provider choices in context

We can help you develop evaluation questions, compare approaches, and review proposals against the workflow. A feature list is only part of that decision. Existing systems, access controls, knowledge maintenance, export options, running costs, and the team's ability to operate the solution all matter.

Where possible, evaluation should use representative work rather than an idealised demonstration. We help clarify which claims a provider needs to substantiate and which assumptions the client needs to test.

Where we can contribute

  • A pilot charter with scope, intended users, dependencies, and decision criteria.
  • Tool and provider evaluation, with a record of trade-offs and open questions.
  • Workflow and integration planning alongside your technical colleagues.
  • Review of evaluation examples, escalation routes, and human review points.
  • Implementation briefings and decision support for the delivery team.
  • Results review and a recommendation to expand, revise, investigate, or stop.

Keep responsibilities visible

In an advisory engagement, your team or implementation partner owns delivery and operation. We provide analysis, challenge, and guidance within an agreed scope. The sponsor owns business decisions, technical owners manage system access and implementation, and workflow owners help establish whether the change works in practice.

We make those responsibilities explicit at the beginning. Advice about a decision is useful when the people who must act on it know who is accountable for the next step.

Review the whole workflow

A technically convincing answer may still create work elsewhere. We look at how information arrives, how exceptions are resolved, how a person takes over, and how changes to the underlying business process reach the system. Training and knowledge maintenance belong in that conversation.

We also help distinguish a correctable implementation issue from a poor fit for the problem. A pilot can produce useful evidence even when the recommendation is to change the approach.

Experience behind the advice

Our dental technology case describes an engagement that progressed from use-case definition and development to a limited live rollout in Canada. That hands-on experience informs the questions we bring to advisory work. Each new engagement has its own agreed responsibilities.
